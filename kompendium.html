Compendia - Client-App

The case-management system with built-in file intelligence — for lawyers who want their evenings back.

IRONSTICK is a complete practice-management system for law firms, on Windows and Android tablets: clients, cases, evidence, deadlines, e-mail and document production, with an AI layer that works on your actual case files. It is pan-European and built for the civil-law tradition, where the codified statute is the authority, and it is available in eleven interface languages: English, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Romanian, Portuguese, Dutch, Turkish, Ukrainian and Russian. IRONSTICK recognises the jurisdiction of each case, builds and maintains its own statute library, and adapts to the way you work. It is offline-first, and every external data flow is documented in the GDPR data-flow declaration.

Who IRONSTICK is made for

IRONSTICK pays off wherever many documents, many parties and hard deadlines come together — and where client data must not leave the building. The firms that gain most are those that lose hours today searching, typing and assembling.

  • Solo lawyers and small firms. No in-house IT required: IRONSTICK runs on a standard Windows PC, runs its own background maintenance and takes on work that would otherwise require additional staff.
  • Litigators with extensive case files. If you handle thousands of pages of evidence, you can query the file in plain language and receive answers with their sources. Court dossiers are built in five guided steps, Bates-numbered and indexed.
  • Commercial, insolvency and criminal lawyers. These proceedings involve many persons, companies and interconnections. The network of persons and companies across all case files makes connections visible that are impractical to track manually — down to OSINT profiles of persons and judge profiles with an evaluation of their decisions.
  • Firms with several lawyers. With IRONSTICK SERVER — the office server — colleagues share cases, one legal library and one network of persons and companies, while each lawyer keeps the complete file on their own workstation.

Part I — The daily practice

1. Your morning, in ninety seconds

Open IRONSTICK and the day is already sorted. The start screen shows what is due and what went quiet; the Agenda merges everything that happened across all your cases — yesterday's filings, overnight e-mails, new court dates, photos, transcripts — into one chronological feed. You see the whole practice without opening a single case. In addition, the separate daily briefing gives you a short spoken overview of the day as you start work.

  • Deadlines with a traffic light. The bell in the menu bar turns orange when a deadline falls due within ten days and red when one is due today or overdue — permanently visible, one click to the full list.
  • E-mail already triaged. The monitor fetches your inbox in the background and assigns messages to cases locally, without AI; the AI rates their urgency. New mail is signalled in the menu bar.
  • Dormant cases and sudden activity. Cases with no activity for 14 days are flagged so they do not go unnoticed; cases with a sudden rise in activity — new deadline, imminent hearing, official mail — carry a flame marker until activity returns to normal.

2. Instant case summaries — ask, don't dig

The case assistant answers questions about your files the way a good associate would — except that it has the entire file indexed and answers in seconds:

  • “Summarise the current state of play in case [case number].”
  • “Which letters came from the opponent, and did we answer the last one?”
  • “Who said what about the site inspection — any contradictions?”
  • “Have I already filed this exhibit with the court?”

Every document an answer cites is a clickable link to the record in your file — numbered results you can open or have read aloud with a short command (“open 1”, “read 1 aloud”); only records retrieved from your database are shown as sources. Routine questions are answered by a local layer on your own machine, without any data leaving the device; heavier analysis escalates to external AI only with your consent. The assistant works in all eleven interface languages and can be operated entirely by voice, hands-free, with offline speech recognition and read-aloud.

The assistant does more than read: it verifies. On request, it validates national ID numbers (Romania, Switzerland, Germany, Austria, France, Italy, Spain), IBANs, VAT numbers (live against the EU VIES system), EORI and LEI numbers — before a wrong number ends up in a pleading.

3. From case file to a submission-ready template — in minutes

This is the flagship. You write pleadings and letters in dialogue with the AI — in IRONSTICK's own chat (on your API key) or through the desktop-AI bridge (Claude, ChatGPT, Qwen or Kimi Desktop). Compose text › With AI help in a case opens the chat with the case and the document already named; from there the AI works like an associate on the live file: client master data, the case chronicle with document full texts, deadlines, related exhibits, the annex list, your case instructions — and even the recent history of the client's neighbouring cases, because the opponent's answer often sits in the parallel file. Where information is missing, it asks follow-up questions instead of forcing your case into fixed templates.

  • Research instead of assertion. While drafting, the AI actively searches the file. It may state that no reply was received only after a search of the file has confirmed it.
  • Verification before hand-over. A deterministic scanner checks the finished draft against the live file: every evidence reference, every party, institution and file number, every statute citation against the verified legal library. Any contradiction blocks the hand-over, and internal file stamps are removed from the output.
  • Red/Blue Team, built in. When switched on, a separate review pass reads the draft as opposing counsel before hand-over: substantial findings send it back for rework, up to two rounds, and each finding is assessed on its merits before it is accepted.
  • A mandatory review chain. Before the long review starts, the AI shows you a summary of the draft — you can still make corrections. It then rereads the whole draft, checks it against your own earlier filings and, for a reply, demonstrates passage by passage that every statement of the other side is answered. After that it examines the pleading from the opposing party's perspective, evaluates it and corrects it; cross-references and time statements are checked as well. IRONSTICK signs off every stage, and any later change starts the chain again from the beginning.
  • A submission-ready template — never a finished filing. The result arrives through IRONSTICK's export dialog and lands in the built-in word processor with one click: A4 pagination, your letterhead (first page or every page), page-break control, signature placement, annex list taken over automatically — print, PDF, DOCX or Markdown. You review, correct and sign; the responsibility for what is filed stays with you.

Hours of file study and drafting become minutes of review — you read, refine and sign.

4. Evidence intelligence — a file that retains every detail

The heart of IRONSTICK is evidence intelligence. Everything you capture — scanned PDFs, e-mails with attachments, WhatsApp histories, photos, audio recordings — becomes searchable full text. OCR is built in, with nothing to install: on Windows it covers more than 35 European languages, including all eleven interface languages. The search is deliberately tolerant: it still finds matches despite missing diacritics, typical scan errors or partial names.

  • Annexes and filings, established from the document itself. When a document is captured, IRONSTICK reads which of your exhibits it physically contains and which reference numbers it carries. The chronicle then shows, on every exhibit: filed with the court on … in case … — and on every outgoing letter, its full annex list. “Did I already file this?” has a one-click answer.
  • Reference-number chains. Each reference number links all documents of the same proceeding into a chronological chain — the documented history of who wrote what, when, and whether it was answered.
  • Photos and recordings as evidence. Photos keep their capture location (one click to the satellite view), phone recordings become speaker-labelled transcripts — all of it searchable, all of it citable evidence.
  • Cross-case awareness. Conflict-of-interest check on intake, warnings when the same opponent appears in several cases, a relationship graph of all parties, and a relevance radar that finds related material you did not think to look for.

Recordings become text — automatically, on your own computer. Phone calls, hearing recordings, voice messages, videos: IRONSTICK accepts every common audio and video format (Windows). You trim the relevant part, choose the language, the number of speakers and the quality level (fast, medium, high) and see the expected computing time before you start. Speech recognition runs entirely offline on your computer. The AI then assigns the sentences to the speakers — with your consent and pseudonymised; it may not change a single word, every line is checked against the recognised text. What recognition did not understand with certainty is marked instead of guessed. The result opens in the editor and, after your review, becomes citable evidence.

The network builds itself. A desktop AI connected via the Bridge reads the file, researches the parties on the internet and files every person, company, institution and connection it finds as a one-click-to-approve proposal; a background auto-linker ties every party to every case whose documents name it; and the Universe turns the whole inventory into a fly-through 3D network with automatically detected clusters — densely connected groups are highlighted in red. Within hours you have a consolidated network of persons and companies across your entire data set, which would not be feasible manually, and the visualisation points straight to the cluster.

What this means in practice — two days, measured. Over two working days in September 2026, a single desktop AI connected through the Bridge read the files of a multi-party proceeding, researched the parties on the internet and filed what it found; the lawyer's role was limited to reviewing and approving the proposals. The result, counted afterwards in the database: 293 persons and companies, 11 institutions, 811 relationships and 733 party-case links — 2,487 review decisions in total, 97 per cent accepted, 3 per cent rejected at the gate. By hand — sources read, every person identified, every connection checked, every record typed — that is about 310 working hours: eight full-time weeks for one person, or half a year alongside ongoing casework. Time is only part of the benefit. No one can keep 300 names and 800 connections in mind; the clusters highlighted in the Universe view cannot be seen in a paper file. The 3 per cent are why the review gate exists: each one would have been a wrong record had it been written without review.

IRONSTICK also draws a client's case map: cases as hexagons, grouped by your own matters, connected where they share parties, cite each other as an exhibit or name the other's case number. The connected AI reads the same map through dedicated tools.

OSINT at the push of a button — built-in research on persons and companies

OSINT (open-source intelligence) — the systematic evaluation of freely accessible sources — is standard practice in investigative authorities and commercial law firms, but it is manual work: everyone researches differently, and everyone forgets something different. IRONSTICK turns it into a fixed procedure. The AI supplies the name, city, district, country and whether the subject is a natural or a legal person; the application then runs the same documented procedure every time:

  • Your own files first. A deterministic full-text search for the name across the entire inventory, all clients: for each case, the ten most recent matches with paragraph and character position, plus a count of further matches.
  • Case-specific research material such as commercial register extracts, land register extracts and the like: file it in the case and instruct the AI to take it into account.
  • Then the internet, on a fixed grid. Career history, social-media profiles (Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram), location, adverse media and press coverage, published declarations of assets and interests, tax information and company holdings — for companies, the commercial register and insolvency records. Search terms are formulated automatically in the language of the subject's country, not in the language of your interface. The research runs automatically in the background in the Client App, and the AI receives a complete OSINT profile to work with, which is more accurate and considerably cheaper than searching step by step.
  • Three search engines at once. Google, DuckDuckGo and Qwant work through the grid in parallel; the results arrive as one merged list without duplicates. If one engine blocks, the others continue.
  • A prepared profile — not a verdict. The AI receives both lists together with the search terms already covered, opens every result and every link one by one, eliminates namesakes and places only what is substantiated as a proposal in the Data Consistency Monitor. Only what you approve is adopted.

The result: a research profile that follows the same procedure and is as complete for the three-hundredth person as for the first. Only publicly accessible sources are evaluated.

What sets IRONSTICK apart

We know of no other product that keeps a full relationship database of persons, companies and institutions at the level of the individual person, breaks it down to the single case file and the single exhibit, presents it clearly to the lawyer and gives the AI the same picture as text — together with a 24-field person analysis for each person; the AI can only propose changes to it, and each one needs your approval.

Conventional practice software typically keeps parties as an address book with a role in the case: no graph between them, no person analysis, no clusters, and its AI assistants read documents, not the network. Most AI legal assistants run in the provider's cloud with a model the firm cannot choose. Large investigation platforms offer graphs and profiles, but they are designed for authorities and corporations and usually require a consulting project; they have no case file in the lawyer's sense and no interchangeable AI that reads them. A firm running criminal and civil proceedings with dozens of parties and interconnected cases falls between these markets. That is where IRONSTICK sits.

Judges and other participants — who is connected to whom, and why

This knowledge does not apply to a single case only. It covers every party, every relationship and every role a person has played in one of your files, across cases and across clients — who sits on which board, who signs for whom, who appeared in which proceeding as witness, opponent or office-holder. It grows every day as a by-product of the work that lawyer and AI do on the files.

  • Relationship analysis. Every connection between persons, companies and institutions is stored with the evidence that supports it; dense networks are detected automatically as clusters. You see not only that two parties are connected, but through what.
  • Judge profiles. Every decision in your files is attributed to the judge or prosecutor who made it — from the heading and the signature block, not by inference. Added to this is the OSINT research on the person: career, public statements, connections to parties and law firms.
  • Judgment evaluation per judge — done once, available for good. How does a particular judge decide, what do they respond to, what do they take up from the pleadings and what do they pass over? Every court decision is recognised on capture — next to incoming/outgoing there is a “court decision” checkbox you can change at any time — and lands on an assessment list; the existing archive is searched automatically as well. The AI works through the list decision by decision: it reads the decision and the submissions of both sides that preceded it, IRONSTICK measures which share of the reasoning comes from whose submission, and the AI answers a fixed grid — arguments, law, evidence, basis of decision, style, outcome and a short characterisation of the panel. Nothing is stored until you approve it, linked to court, judges and court clerks; if one of these persons is missing from the master data, they are first researched via OSINT and created. A decision does not change, so it is assessed once and the assessment remains.
  • Pleadings informed by the court's practice. A pleading can take into account the individual judge or the panel that will read it — composition, prior decisions and positions, documented connections to the parties and, where present, grounds for recusal — because the documented connections and decision history are available to you. Before every pleading to a court, the AI reads the collected assessments of that court and of every judge on the panel. If decisions are still unassessed, IRONSTICK asks before the output whether you want to assess them now — so the result still flows in — or later; and at the first start of the day the AI reminds you of its own accord.
  • Making coordination visible. Any two pleadings, even from different proceedings and clients, can be compared word for word: identical text blocks are found automatically, even across OCR errors, and can indicate coordination between counsel.

In court that is the difference between presenting a case and knowing the field: every document can be aligned with the actual network around the case — who is connected to whom, and who previously appeared on which side — because the lawyer knows where everything is. Compiling this manually is impractical; in IRONSTICK it builds up as part of daily case work.

One sheet instead of a thousand lines. The relationship network of a person or a case and the chronology of its documents are ready as a chart at the press of a button: the network shows the fifty most urgent nodes, weighted by deadlines, recent activity and connections; the chronology lists incoming and outgoing letters in time order, with the annexes right beside each letter. Every number in it is clickable and leads to the evidence, the person or the institution. One click on Capture takes the chart to document capture, where you assign it and it receives its stamp when saved; from the case network you can also create a Wallpaper: a printable A4 landscape overview for the office wall; it is downloaded only and is not stored in the case file.

The desktop AI connected through the Bridge draws in the same design too: processes, procedural paths, chronologies, networks. It discusses the chart with you and shows the preview in IRONSTICK; drawing and the error check before display are deterministic, without AI. Charts can also be viewed on the tablet.

5. A document system built for court

IRONSTICK is also a full DMS — one that thinks in exhibits, stamps and page numbers rather than folders:

  • Capture with AI pre-fill. Drop a scanned PDF into the inbox: OCR runs, the AI proposes title, type, parties, case assignment, date and deadline, and IRONSTICK additionally recognises the matching case on its own — you confirm instead of typing. Every captured file gets a cryptographic integrity fingerprint (SHA-256), so any later alteration can be detected.
  • Stamping & pagination. Merging, Bates numbering, file stamps, metadata cleaning — every produced PDF carries a searchable text layer, so the finished document can be searched and quoted without ever re-running OCR.
  • The dossier assistant. A guided, five-step path from chronicle to a submission-ready case dossier: choose entries, cover sheet and jurisdiction, preview with page thumbnails, then a finished PDF with table of contents and Bates numbers — plus an EDRM evidence ZIP for eDiscovery hand-over.
  • Redaction that holds. Category switches (clients, names, places, amounts, ID numbers, IBANs …), a local finder that marks candidates, manual marking on the page — and on export the redactions are burned in irreversibly, with a clean searchable text layer over the top.
  • Mail dispatch for oversized files. A large PDF is split losslessly into parts that fit the size limits of court e-filing mailboxes, each with a ready-made delivery e-mail in the document's language — copy, attach, send.

Large-scale filings — 3,000 pages, stamped and paginated, in under 20 minutes

In civil procedure and large-scale litigation this goes straight to the biggest pain points of lawyers and their staff — time, error rate and manual labour:

  • Time savings. Work that otherwise falls to a paralegal or the lawyer — assembling and merging PDFs by hand, checking pages, stamping every page manually (Bates numbering) — runs fully automatically here.
  • Consistency and fewer errors. With more than 2,000 pages, manual work quickly produces mistakes: misplaced stamps, wrong page numbers on annexes, missing links, missed formatting rules. An automated pipeline applies the same stamping and pagination rules to every page.
  • Stable with very large files. Standard office software and PDF viewers often become unresponsive with a 700 MB file of 3,000 pages as soon as you try to work through it or re-paginate it.
  • Speed under deadline pressure. Shortly before a filing deadline, large pleadings with all their evidence are ready as a submission-ready template within minutes.
Tested. 3,000 pages of pleadings, annexes mixed text and image, stamped and paginated, output one 700 MB PDF — in under 20 minutes.

Part II — AI at full depth

6. Your desktop AI, plugged straight into your file

For deep analytical work, IRONSTICK offers something no export folder can: a live bridge between your own desktop AI — Claude Desktop, ChatGPT Desktop, Qwen Desktop and/or Kimi Desktop, each licensed separately and unlocked in your Client App licence key — and the running application. The AI reads the current file state through more than 200 purpose-built tools — chronicles, full texts, e-mails, deadlines, master data — and works at full depth:

  • “Read the whole case and tell me where our position is weakest.”
  • “Draft the appeal in our pleading format and hand it over to the word processor.” — the draft appears in IRONSTICK, ready for letterhead printing.
  • “Compare the three expert opinions and build me a contradiction table.”

Built for large files — why a live bridge beats any file export. The bridge add-ons are designed for one purpose: working on large data pools that demand analysis at full detail. You keep full control through switches, consent gates and per-AI licensing, while every conversation and every evaluation can draw on the entire IRONSTICK data pool. This level of detail cannot be achieved through a conventional file feed (exporting Markdown files into an AI chat): even the largest AI context windows hold only a few megabytes of text at once —

Why a language model alone cannot handle large case files — and how IRONSTICK solves this

A language model on its own is outmatched by a mature case file, and, contrary to a widespread assumption, a bigger context window (1 or 2 million tokens) does not fix that:

  • The “lost in the middle” phenomenon. Give a model hundreds of raw pages at once and its accuracy on details drops sharply: facts from the middle of the file are “forgotten” or hallucinated.
  • Context overload. A single tool result that pours 50,000 characters of full text into the chat overloads the conversation: the model loses orientation, the context overflows, and everything becomes slow and expensive.
  • No memory, no structure. A language model on its own knows nothing of deadlines, file numbers, parties, Bates stamps or the chronology of proceedings. It sees one long string of characters.

IRONSTICK does not use the language model to carry the whole file; it gives it precise, structured access:

  1. Structure first. Before the AI sees a single word, IRONSTICK has already structured the file: documents classified, dates indexed, OCR repaired, annexes linked.
  2. Decoupling through tools and a work area. Through more than 200 specialised tools the AI fetches precisely what the question needs — and when a result is very large after all, anything above a fixed size limit is automatically diverted to a local, AES-256-encrypted work area that only IRONSTICK can read. The chat stays uncluttered.
  3. Persistence. Through the shared AI memory, the automatic daily logs and the work area, analysis results are kept across sessions: the AI builds knowledge over weeks instead of starting from zero with every prompt.

The bottom line: a language model without a system is like a brilliant lawyer handed 5,000 loose, unsorted pages and told to find the decisive line in five seconds. IRONSTICK is the infrastructure that turns that lawyer into a functioning high-performance practice.

Set-up is one button per AI in the configuration (for Qwen Desktop, IRONSTICK copies the set-up text for you to paste into Qwen's own dialog); for Claude and ChatGPT, a skill plug-in gives the AI your firm's mandatory output formats. What the AI learns about a case is kept in a shared AI memory — compact, client-bound and visible to you; the AI can propose to store, change or delete an entry, and each change takes effect only after you approve it. And you always see what is happening: whenever an AI reads your file, its symbol lights up in the menu bar.

Websites with an access check. If a website requires a confirmation before it releases its content, IRONSTICK opens the page in a separate window: you confirm there, and work on the page continues seamlessly.

The first access in each program run requires your explicit consent; documents marked secret are invisible on this channel; changes the AI proposes to your master data wait for your approval in the Data Consistency Monitor. Chronicle entries arrive as a prefilled capture form — you add photos if you like and save it yourself.
The bridges run in parallel. If several add-ons are licensed, Claude, ChatGPT, Qwen and Kimi can be connected at the same time and even work on the same case simultaneously — sharing the same file state, the same AI memory and the same review gates, while the menu bar shows one pulsing logo per active AI, so every access is attributed to the AI that made it.

Several minds, one case — a built-in board of advisers

This goes well beyond a chatbot. Because the bridges can run at once, you put independent AIs on the same file and let them check each other's work — with IRONSTICK in the middle as shared database and coordinator. The decisive point: Claude, ChatGPT, Qwen and Kimi are different models with different training, so you get genuinely different opinions and approaches — a real second opinion, not one model nodding at itself. These are among the leading AI models available today; IRONSTICK puts them, side by side, on your case.

You direct them in plain language, like a partner steering two associates: “Work this out … now give it to the other one to check … bring me its assessment … revise.” The draft, theory or pleading is handed over through IRONSTICK, the second AI reviews it critically — tasked to find weaknesses, counter-arguments and errors, not to praise — and its assessment comes back to you. Both work on the identical case data, and the entire exchange is logged and traceable.

Multi-model review is particularly useful for:

  • Developing theories of the case — build one, have the other stress-test it.
  • Pattern recognition across the file — two models catch what one misses.
  • Working out tactics and strategy — proposal and counter-proposal.
  • Drafting and hardening pleadings — write, review, rebut, refine.
  • Cross-checking your general lines of reasoning — before the opponent does.

A file that repairs itself — with you in the loop

Capture errors and poor scans no longer have to be permanent. When the AI encounters a stored full text that is truncated or damaged by a poor OCR run, it does not quietly work around it — it repairs the record: it requests a fresh, page-accurate re-scan of the original PDF (you pick the pages in the familiar page picker), compares the new scan with the stored text passage by passage, and is instructed to repair only mechanical OCR errors, without rewording and without changing numbers, names or amounts. It then hands the corrected full text back. You see it in full, and the corrected text replaces the old one only after you approve it. The archive becomes cleaner with every week you work in it.

Where even a fresh scan reaches physical limits, the AI asks you specifically: instead of a generic “file could not be processed”, it opens the entry, names the exact spot (“page 12, near the top”) and asks you to type the illegible words, each with page and position — or to hand it the original PDF via the paperclip so it can read the scan with its own vision. You never comb through a 200-page file; you are called in only for the passages the machine cannot resolve with certainty. Conventional systems report either success or a generic error; IRONSTICK instead combines AI speed with a targeted human check.

Part III — Verification and sovereignty

7. The hallucination firewall — deterministic verification gates

The one justified fear about AI in legal work: models invent citations, misquote figures, and claim to have read files they merely skimmed. The industry answers with prompts and disclaimers — IRONSTICK answers with architecture. Your desktop AI works on your file through more than 200 purpose-built tools, and every path to an outgoing document leads through deterministic gates: pure logic, no AI checking AI.

  • Read proof. The system records, line by line, what the AI actually read of your file. Unread passages are flagged in every answer — and delivery stays locked while gaps exist. “I read everything” stops being a claim and becomes a measurement.
  • Citation provenance. Every evidence reference must point to a real record the AI demonstrably fetched and fully read in this working session — citing from search snippets or from memory fails verification.
  • Statutes only from a verified library. Cited articles are checked against the letter of the law stored in your own verified library; new sources enter it only through your consent dialog. An invented article number blocks delivery.
  • Zero tolerance for numbers and names. Amounts must match the record exactly, party names down to the diacritic; IBANs and Romanian personal numbers (CNP) run through checksum validators, and a number that fails its checksum blocks delivery.
  • Delivery only against attestation. A formal pleading is refused unless a fresh verification of exactly that file state came back green — every edit voids the attestation, and five failed rounds abort the loop and notify you. Every delivered document carries its verification stamp.
  • No silent changes after delivery. A correction to a delivered document is not applied silently: the old version is visibly voided, and a new version is created. Your corrections feed into the review.
  • No side doors. A letter declared “informal” is content-checked and rerouted through the formal gates; documents cannot be renamed to reset their version history; statute knowledge from press articles or the model's memory fails verification — only the law's own text, demonstrably read from the library in this session, grounds a citation; and every answer about a case carries the case's true subject from your records, so any mix-up with another case is immediately apparent.
  • Quotes across case boundaries. If a quote comes from a document of another case, the verifier finds it across the entire archive and names the exact document — the AI reads exactly that one, not the whole foreign file.
  • Origin of every document. For every document, the AI learns where it comes from: if it comes from the opposing side or an authority, its statements must first be critically examined; if it comes from the client, it reflects the client's view.
  • Protection against injected instructions. Web pages can contain text addressed to the AI rather than to you. Before web content reaches the AI, IRONSTICK removes hidden bait text and checks the rest against a pattern catalogue in eleven languages. Detected passages are made illegible and the AI receives a notice — pure logic, no AI checking an AI.
  • No repetition at your expense. If a desktop AI tries to reload data it already holds unchanged, the call is refused — your AI budget and time go into the work, not into repetition.
And you are not tied to one AI vendor. The built-in AI runs on Qwen, Kimi, ChatGPT and Claude — four identically structured sections in the configuration. IRONSTICK also keeps costs down automatically: every provider you have set up is used automatically, lowest cost first — Qwen, then Kimi, ChatGPT, Claude. At every start the program checks which of them respond; if a quota or credit runs out during the day, it switches automatically to the next configured provider and shows a warning symbol in the menu bar; the next day it checks again. No selector, no interruption, no lost work. The desktop side offers Claude, ChatGPT, Qwen and Kimi. The classic way

Most AI assistants still have no connection to the database that holds your cases and client data — the only way in is uploading files. Many cloud-based systems work the same way: at most, they move text files around in the background, invisible to the user. The AI then draws only on the information in exactly these uploaded files. If they are not too large, the AI may take everything into account; otherwise hallucinations become likely. The AI cannot access the case itself: it works without sight of the file and tries to get the best result for the user. That can lead to serious misjudgements and false statements.

Additional skills and plug-ins do not change this. They can even make it worse: their extensive instructions take up a considerable part of the context window before the AI has read the first document — and the fuller that window is, the more unevenly the AI takes into account what is in it.

An AI model has a limited working memory. If it is flooded with large amounts of material at once, it cannot retain everything and may lose important facts or instructions.

That is what happens today with “best effort” AI offerings, however they are advertised. In practice, the AI’s context is simply filled with your case data: entire dossiers, long texts and, on top of that, long instructions and possibly plug-ins and skills. The context fills until nothing more fits, and is then compressed or only partly read. The AI is then asked to draft your pleading on that basis, and the risk of error is high.

IRONSTICK takes a different path. Instructions are capped at 20,000 characters, a small fraction of the context window of the supported models. Your case data is first prepared by IRONSTICK, and the AI receives only the data that matters (compact, structured data without prose). On the way, the AI is not left alone: it is given tools and safeguards, and it is guided to the goal by signposts and verification gates.

The difference?

Classic best-effort offerings first give the AI a long set of instructions, then rely on it to follow them and not to have forgotten anything.

IRONSTICK guides the AI continuously, with checkpoints, additional information and tools along the way, and checks both the path and the result of the AI model throughout.

Independent of the vendor. Your case file is stored on your own hardware, the licence is checked offline on your computer, and IRONSTICK needs no vendor server to work.
